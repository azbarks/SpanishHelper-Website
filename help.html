🚀 Getting Started

Spanish Helper teaches the words and phrases you'll hear and say most in everyday Mexican Spanish. Each word you're studying comes back to you — on a schedule, or in a line — until you know it both ways, then it steps aside so you can learn the next one.

Your first day

  1. Mark what you already know (optional, but worth it). On the Today tab, tap Sort the Word Bank. It shows the most common words one at a time — tap I Know It for the ones you know and Learn It for the ones you want to study. See Sorting the Word Bank.
  2. Or just start. Scroll to Learn Something New on Today and tap Start 5 new words. They're the next most common words you haven't started.
  3. Answer each card. Tap a card to see the answer and hear it, then tap ✓ if you knew it or ✗ if you didn't.
  4. Come back tomorrow. A few minutes a day beats an hour once a week.
A good pace: add about 5 new words a day, and keep your No Schedule line between 50 and 100 words. The line tells you how often each word comes around — if that number climbs past two weeks, slow down on new words for a few days.

🎯 Why these words

Many courses teach by topic — the airport, the office, even "HVAC maintenance." Spanish Helper starts instead with the words people actually use most. A few hundred common words make up most of everyday conversation, so every word you learn here pays off right away.

  • Mexican Spanish only. Words, notes and the voice are Mexican — carro, camión, platicar, ahorita — and the app uses ustedes, never vosotros.
  • Most common first. The Word Bank is in frequency order, so "Learn Something New" always gives you the most useful words you don't know yet.
  • Present tense first. Example sentences skip the past tense until you're ready (you can turn that off). "Going to" — voy a comer, "I'm going to eat" — is included, because it's the easy, everyday way to talk about the future.

The 🎧 headphones button at the top opens Listen.

✅ Right or missed

Each card shows one side — Spanish or English, depending on its step. Say the answer to yourself (out loud is even better), then tap the card to check. It shows the meaning, an example sentence and any notes, and says the Spanish.

✓ Got it or ✗ Missed it
  • ✓ adds a day to its streak — the dots under the card. Only one correct answer a day counts: four taps in one sitting isn't four days of remembering.
  • ✗ starts the streak over and puts the word on the Missed list for another try. A word on a schedule also moves one step more often.

A scheduled word you got right shows a green check for the rest of the day — tap it to undo if you tapped by mistake.

Be honest. If you hesitated for a long time or only half-remembered it, tap ✗. The app uses your answers to decide when to show each word again — a generous ✓ just means you'll see it less before you really know it.

🚶 The No Schedule line

New words start on No Schedule. Instead of a date, they wait in a line — one line for words and one for phrases. Today shows the next 10 words and 5 phrases (you can change that in Settings).

  • Answer a card and it leaves the page; the next one in line slides in. You can keep going through the whole line if you have time.
  • If you stop partway, tomorrow starts right where you left off — a bookmark, not a restart.
  • The header shows where the bookmark is: Word 11 of 40 means today starts with the 11th word of 40 in your line.
  • The footer is the line gauge: "40 in the line · each comes around about every 4 days." It turns orange past two weeks — a sign to slow down on new words.
  • When you've gone through the whole line, it says All 40 done for today, with a Go Around Again button if you want more.
  • New words you start join the line right after the bookmark, so they come up next.
Answer in order. The bookmark only moves past words you've answered. If you don't want to see a word for a while, swipe it away (Pause or Learned) instead of skipping over it.

📅 Schedules

Any word can instead be put on a schedule — useful for words that keep giving you trouble (Daily) or ones you nearly know (Weekly).

ScheduleComes up
DailyEvery day
Every 2 / 3 / 4 DaysOn its day, every few days
WeeklyOnce a week
Every 2 WeeksTwice a month
MonthlyOnce a month
  • Swipe left on a card for More Often (or Daily, for a No Schedule word); swipe right on a scheduled card for Less Often. Long-press → Schedule to pick any one, or go back to No Schedule.
  • Missed a day? Nothing piles up. A word that wasn't answered on its day simply waits for its next turn — so a week away doesn't leave you with a mountain of reviews.
  • Load balancing spreads words with the same schedule across different days: set 20 words to Every 4 Days and you'll see about 5 a day, not 20 at once.
  • Automatic spacing (off unless you turn it on in Settings) moves a scheduled word one step less often each time you get it right.

↔️ Two learning steps

Recognizing a word and being able to say it are different skills, so every word goes through two steps:

StepYou seeYou do
Step 1The SpanishRemember what it means
Step 2The EnglishSay it in Spanish

The line under each card shows its progress — Step 1 · 2 of 4 days and a row of dots. After 4 correct days in a row (you can change the number), the app asks — it never moves a word on its own:

  • At the end of Step 1: Yes — English First, Mark It Learned (if it's easy), or Keep Studying.
  • At the end of Step 2: Mark It Learned, Not Yet (keep studying, but less often), or Keep Studying.

Keep Studying keeps half your progress (4 of 4 becomes 2 of 4), so it asks again after a couple more good days.

Missing a Step 2 word starts its count over but keeps it English first. If you miss it three times, the app asks whether to put it on Daily, pause it, or go Back to Spanish First. You can also switch a word's step any time from the long-press menu.

Already know a word? Swipe left → Learned and skip the steps entirely.

🔁 Missed — Try Again

Words you miss go to the Missed — Try Again list near the bottom of Today, oldest first. Get one right there and it leaves the list. It's there for a second look whenever you want one — and each word still comes around again on its normal turn.

If you miss the same word on three different days, the app asks whether to Put It on Daily, Pause It for a Month, or Leave It.

🌱 The Refresher

Learned words leave your daily study — but they're not gone. Each day the Refresher brings back 3 of them (change the number in Settings), the ones you haven't seen in longest, so over time it cycles through everything you've learned. Learned words are shown English first, the harder test.

Miss a Refresher word and the app asks:

  • Study It Again — it goes back on your list, English first. Since you knew it once, one correct day brings back the "learned?" question.
  • Keep It Learned — it stays Learned, but comes first in the Refresher every day until you get it right. Then it goes back to the normal rotation.

On a Refresher card, swipe right for Still Know It, or swipe left to Study Again or Never Remind me.

📊 This Week

Tap the This Week row on Today to see words learned this week, right and missed answers, days practiced, your streak, how many words you're studying, your Trickiest Words (missed most this week) and the words Closest to Learned.

Coming Up, at the bottom of Today, counts the scheduled words for each of the next 7 days. No Schedule words aren't counted there — they don't have dates.

🗂 Sorting the Word Bank

If you already know some Spanish, sorting saves you from studying words you know. Tap Sort the Word Bank on Today (or the Sort button on the Words tab). Words come up one at a time, most common first; tap to see the meaning, then choose:

ButtonWhat happens
I Know ItMarked Learned. The Refresher will check on it now and then.
Know It, Never RemindFor words you've known for years (uno, dos, hola) — it never comes up again.
Learn ItStarts studying it.
Not NowLeaves it in the Word Bank for later.

Undo takes back the last choice. To keep a word from ever being offered, long-press it → Not Interested.

💬 Phrases & examples

Phrases are where words come alive, so the app makes them easy to collect. When you check a word's card you'll see an example sentence with two buttons:

  • Add to My Phrases — the sentence becomes a phrase you're studying, next up in your phrase line. A moment later the next example for that word appears, so you can add another.
  • Another example — see the next sentence without adding this one.

The most common words and every verb have several examples, mixing I, you, he/she, we and they. The 💬+ bubble next to sentences in Talk and Stories adds those too. The app never adds the same phrase twice.

➕ Your own words

Heard a word you want to learn? On the Words tab, tap +, choose Word or Phrase, and fill in the Spanish and English (an example and notes are optional). It starts on No Schedule, next up. If it's already in the app, Spanish Helper tells you where instead of making a duplicate.

You can also edit any built-in word — fix a meaning, add your own note. Updates to the app never overwrite your edits.

🔀 Verbs

The Verbs tab has conjugation tables for all 236 verbs in the app. Start with How Spanish verbs work at the top — a short, plain-English explanation of endings and what to learn first.

  • Filter by All, Irregular, Regular or Studying, and search.
  • Each verb shows Present, Past, Near future (voy a + verb), Future and Present perfect, for yo, tú, él / ella / usted, nosotros, ustedes and ellos / ellas. Irregular forms are in orange.
  • Quiz Me (the eye button) hides every form until you tap it.
  • A verb's card on Today has a Verb endings button that opens its tables.

🗣 Talk

The Talk tab is the step from flashcards to conversation.

Ask Today

Three simple questions a day to ask someone who speaks Spanish — a spouse, a friend, a neighbor — each with a follow-up line. Tap one to hear it. Show 3 More gives you another three. A green line lists the words in it that you're studying.

Dialogues

Short conversations for everyday situations: meeting a neighbor, ordering at a restaurant, the market, a taxi, the pharmacy, asking directions, and when you don't understand. Three ways to practice:

  • Listen — hear both sides.
  • My Part — your lines show in English; the app pauses so you can say them in Spanish, then says them so you can check.
  • Other Part — the same, playing the other person.

Watch

A link to Dreaming Spanish on YouTube — free videos in easy Spanish by native speakers, sorted by level. Start with the Superbeginner videos.

📖 Stories

Short, easy stories to listen to and read — 57 in all, in three levels. Level 1 uses only the present tense and "going to"; Level 2 adds the past; Level 3 is longer and mixes tenses.

  • Each story shows You know N% — how many of its words you're studying or have learned. The higher it is, the more you'll understand.
  • The Talk tab shows your shelf of a few stories at a time. Give Me Another Story adds the one with the most words you know, at the level you choose.
  • In a story, tap ▶ to hear it, tap any sentence to hear it again, and turn on English at the top when you need it. Words you know are in green.
  • Done with one? Swipe left → Finished (or Not for Me), or tap I'm Done with This Story at the bottom. Finished keeps them, and All Stories lists every story by level.
Try this: listen first without reading, then read along, and only then peek at the English.

💾 Your data

Everything stays on your iPhone. There's no account and nothing is sent anywhere. It is not synced through iCloud, so a backup is the way to move to a new phone.

  • Export Backup saves a file of all your words, schedules and progress — plus where you are in each line, your This Week history, your settings and your story shelf. Send it to Files, AirDrop or email.
  • Restore from Backup merges a backup in: words in the backup take its progress, new ones are added, and nothing is deleted.
  • Restore Missing Built-in Words adds back any of the built-in words that aren't on your phone.
  • Reset All Progress sends every word back to the Word Bank. Your own words and edits are kept. Export a backup first!

❓ FAQ

I skipped a few days. Do I have a pile of reviews now?

No. A scheduled word that wasn't answered on its day just waits for its next turn, and your No Schedule line picks up at the bookmark. Come back whenever you can.

Should I put new words on Daily or leave them on No Schedule?

Leave them on No Schedule — that's what it's for. With a line of 50–100 words, each comes back every few days. Put a word on Daily only when it keeps slipping away.

Should I learn words first or phrases?

Both, together. Words give you the pieces; phrases show how they fit. A good habit: when you check a word you're learning, add one of its example sentences as a phrase.

Why did a word come up English first?

It's in Step 2, or it's a Learned word in the Refresher. See Two learning steps. Long-press a card to switch its step.

I got a word right four days in a row — why didn't it move on?

The app asks before moving a word on. If you tapped Keep Studying, it kept half your progress and will ask again after a couple more correct days.

Where are the past-tense examples?

Hidden while Settings → Example Sentences → No past tense yet is on. Turn it off when you're ready. The Verbs tab always shows the past tense tables.

Is this Spain Spanish or Mexican Spanish?

Mexican, throughout — the words, the notes, the example sentences and the voice. There's no vosotros; like Mexico, the app uses ustedes for "you all."

Does it work without internet?

Yes. Everything — including the Spanish voice — works offline. Only the Dreaming Spanish link needs the internet.

How do I move to a new iPhone?

Settings → Backup → Export Backup on the old phone and save the file to Files or AirDrop it. On the new phone, Restore from Backup and pick the file.

A word or translation looks wrong.

Tap the word on the Words tab and fix it — your edit is kept. And please let me know so I can fix it for everyone.
